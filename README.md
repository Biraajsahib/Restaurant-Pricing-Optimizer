# Restaurant Pricing Intelligence & Promotion Optimizer

## Project Overview

The Restaurant Pricing Intelligence & Promotion Optimizer is an end-to-end machine learning and Generative AI decision-support application that helps a multi-outlet restaurant brand decide **what to charge for each menu item and which offer to run**.

It combines sales history, machine learning, explainable AI, optimization and Generative AI in one interactive Streamlit application.

The core workflow is:

**Predict → Optimize → Explain → Decide**

---

## Business Problem

A restaurant manager makes these decisions every week:

- What should the menu price of each item be?
- Should we run a discount, a combo or a buy-one-get-one offer?
- On which days should offers run?
- How many plates will we sell, and what will it do to revenue and profit?

A strategy that sells more plates does not necessarily earn more.

> Higher demand ≠ Higher profit

A buy-one-get-one offer can lift orders sharply while the free plate wipes out the margin. A discount on a busy Saturday gives away money to guests who would have come anyway. This project therefore maximizes **expected profit**, not units sold.

---

## Project Objective

Build a decision-support system that can:

1. Predict demand for a menu item at an outlet on a given day.
2. Evaluate different menu-price and offer scenarios.
3. Estimate revenue, cost and expected profit for each.
4. Identify the most profitable strategy.
5. Explain the main drivers of demand.
6. Answer natural-language pricing questions.

## Machine Learning

### Demand Prediction

The final demand model is an XGBoost regression model stored at `models/xgboost_demand_model.pkl`.

The saved file is a scikit-learn Pipeline (preprocessor + XGBoost model) that expects **37 input features**.

### Feature Engineering

| Group | Features |
|---|---|
| Time | `Month`, `Quarter`, `Week`, `Day_of_Month`, `Day_of_Week`, `Is_Weekend` |
| Cyclical | `Month_Sin`, `Month_Cos`, `Week_Sin`, `Week_Cos` |
| Price | `Price_Index`, `Competitor_Price_Gap`, `Competitor_Price_Ratio` |
| Offer | `Promotion_Type`, `Promotion_Flag`, `Promotion_Depth`, `Discount_Percentage` |
| History | `Avg_Item_Demand`, `Avg_Item_Price` |

`Avg_Item_Demand` is the average past demand of the item at that outlet. It uses only days **before** the day being predicted, so it cannot leak the answer.

### Price rule built into the model

Predicted demand is never allowed to go **up** when our own price goes up (XGBoost `monotone_constraints` on the four price features). Without this rule a tree model can learn random bumps from noisy data and the optimizer would chase them. Notebook 3 checks the rule on 2,000 test rows: zero violations.

### Model Performance

Trained on the first 80% of dates, tested on the last 20% (chronological split).

| Model | MAE | RMSE | R² |
|---|---|---|---|
| **XGBoost** | 4.632 | 6.320 | 0.938 |
| Random Forest | 4.970 | 6.811 | 0.928 |
| Linear Regression | 6.364 | 8.927 | 0.876 |

XGBoost has the highest R² and the lowest error, and is used as the final model. Results are saved to `data/model_performance_summary.csv`.

## Price & Offer Optimization

The optimizer evaluates:

**5 menu-price levels × 7 offers = 35 candidate scenarios**

Menu-price levels: −10%, −5%, 0, +5%, +10% of the base price. These are the levels present in the sales history, so the model is never asked about a price it has not seen.

### Offers

| Offer | Customer pays | Extra cost to the restaurant |
|---|---|---|
| No Promotion | menu price | none |
| 5% / 10% / 15% / 20% Discount | menu price less the discount | none (the discount is the cost) |
| Combo Offer | 90% of menu price, with an add-on | 6% of the item's base price per unit |
| Buy One Get One | menu price, for two plates | the food cost of the free plate |

Offer cost is **item-specific**: buy-one-get-one on a ₹55 chai costs far less than on a ₹445 biryani.

### Guardrail: no fake discounts

An offer is only allowed on a menu price at or below the base price. Raising the menu price by 10% and then advertising "10% off" would be a fake discount, so those 12 combinations are excluded. **23 valid strategies** remain.

### Profit Calculation

```text
Revenue          = Price the customer pays × Predicted units
Food cost        = Unit food cost × Predicted units
Promotion cost   = Promotion cost per unit × Predicted units
Total cost       = Food cost + Promotion cost + Overhead
Expected profit  = Revenue − Total cost
```

The valid scenario with the highest expected profit is the recommended strategy.

### What the optimizer finds

Run across the whole menu (240 item-outlet pairs, Notebook 4):

- Expected profit rises **22.6% on a Tuesday** and **5.8% on a Saturday**, compared with selling at base price with no offer.
- **Offers belong on slow days.** "No Promotion" is the best choice for 8% of item-outlet pairs on Tuesday but 57% on Saturday.
- **Different categories need different offers.** On a Tuesday the most recommended offer is Combo Offer for Main Course and Rice & Biryani, Buy One Get One for Beverages, a discount for Starters and Desserts, and no offer for Breads.
- **Weekends can carry a higher price.** 50% of item-outlet pairs can take a higher menu price on Saturday, against 7% on Tuesday.

## Generative AI Pricing Advisor

The AI Pricing Advisor is powered by Google's Gemini API. Gemini is **not** the forecasting model: it only explains numbers that Python and XGBoost have already calculated.

```text
User question → Python scenario calculation → XGBoost demand prediction
             → Revenue / cost / profit calculation → Gemini → Business explanation
```

Example questions:

```text
What will happen if I increase the price by 10%?
Is BOGO profitable?
Which offer is best?
Why is the recommended strategy best?
Compare combo and 10% discount.
What drives demand?
```

If no Gemini key is set, or the request fails, the advisor shows a built-in summary of the same numbers, so the dashboard keeps working.

## Important Modeling Considerations

**Synthetic data.** The results show that the method works; the actual numbers would change with a real restaurant's data.

**Predictive, not causal.** The model learns patterns in the sales history. In this dataset prices and offers were varied independently of demand conditions, which is what makes their effect measurable. Real data needs the same care (planned price tests).

**Price range.** The model has seen menu prices within ±10% of the base price. The dashboard warns when a price outside that range is entered.

**Edge of the range.** When the recommended price is +10% or −10%, the true best price may lie beyond the tested range.

**Small differences.** Profit changes slowly near the best price, so two neighbouring price levels often differ by very little. Treat the recommendation as a direction, not an exact rupee figure.

**Expected conditions.** For an upcoming date, footfall and weather are historical averages for that weekday, festival and month.

**One item at a time.** The optimizer does not model one item's offer taking sales from another item, kitchen or seating capacity, or offers losing their effect when run permanently.

**GenAI.** Gemini is an explanation layer. All demand and profit numbers come from the analytical pipeline.
